How the Analysis Was Structured

We flattened the labeled games into player-level, window-level, and game-level tables so the KOL-target relationship could be tested from multiple angles.
2 / 5
Main hypothesis
  • Werewolves do not distribute persuasion evenly across the table.
  • They disproportionately orient their persuasion toward the current discussion leader or KOL.
  • If that is true, players with higher discussion_leader_count should also have higher werewolf_target_count.
Level Key Variables
Player discussion_leader_count, influence, werewolf_target_count, werewolf_target_rank
Window discussion_leader, targeted_player, target_is_kol, target_is_leader
Game kol_player, kol_target_rank, windows_targeting_kol, leader-target overlap rate
The analysis treats KOL as a game-level emergent role derived from discussion leadership rather than a fixed role card.

Leadership and Targeting Move Together

The core player-level pattern is a strong positive relationship between how often a player leads discussion and how often the Werewolf targets them.
3 / 5
Figure 1. The fitted trend line and clustered high-value points show that players who lead discussion more often are also more frequently chosen as Werewolf targets.
Interpretation
  • Pearson r = 0.753 and Spearman ρ = 0.783, which is a strong positive association.
  • This is consistent with a strategy where Werewolves concentrate persuasion on players who can shape the rest of the table.
  • The relationship is not just linear noise: rank correlation is similarly high, suggesting the pattern is robust even when target counts are not normally distributed.
Player-level evidence: discussion leadership is a strong predictor of Werewolf targeting frequency.

Game-Level Evidence and Takeaways

The KOL is usually the highest-priority Werewolf target, and discussion-leader targeting appears repeatedly across games rather than in a few isolated cases.
5 / 5
Figure 3. In 76% of games, the KOL had target rank 1. Only one game placed the KOL at rank 3.
Figure 4. Across games, many persuasion windows directly align the targeted player with the current discussion leader.
Takeaways for the paper
  • KOL targeting is a dominant Werewolf strategy. In most games, the KOL is the top-ranked persuasion target.
  • Leadership is a behavioral cue for strategic pressure. The stronger a player’s leadership signal, the more likely the Werewolf is to orient persuasion toward them.
  • Windows often collapse target and leader. The targeted player is frequently the same person who is currently steering interpretation at the table.
This supports a persuasion model where Werewolves try to influence the player most capable of shifting collective belief, rather than appealing uniformly to the group.
Recommended headline: “Werewolves preferentially target key opinion leaders rather than the table at large.”
